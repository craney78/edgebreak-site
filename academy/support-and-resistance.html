What is support?

Support is a price area where buying interest has historically been strong enough to slow or temporarily stop a decline. When price reaches support, some market participants may view the area as more attractive and demand can increase.

Support does not guarantee that price will move higher. It simply marks an area where buyers have previously influenced price behaviour.

What is resistance?

Resistance is the opposite of support. It represents a price area where selling interest has repeatedly been strong enough to prevent price from moving higher.

As price approaches resistance, some market participants may choose to sell while others wait to see whether demand is strong enough to absorb that supply.

Resistance is an important concept throughout EdgeBreak because a breakout is defined by price moving beyond a previously established resistance area.

Support and resistance are not predictions. They are reference areas that help you understand where price has repeatedly reacted.
Support An area below price Historically associated with increased buying interest or reduced selling pressure.
Resistance An area above price Historically associated with increased selling interest or reduced buying momentum.

Why does resistance form?

Resistance often develops because many market participants are willing to sell within a similar price area. That supply can come from profit-taking, previous buyers exiting positions or larger participants managing exposure.

When price repeatedly reacts around the same area, the market begins to recognise it as a meaningful reference zone.

The more often the area is tested and observed, the more attention it may attract from traders and investors.

Why multiple resistance tests matter

One rejection at resistance provides limited information. Repeated tests of the same area can be more useful because they show that buyers continue returning to the zone despite earlier rejections.

That does not mean a breakout must occur. Instead, repeated testing gives you more evidence about persistence, price compression and how the balance between supply and demand may be changing.

If demand eventually becomes strong enough to absorb the available supply, price may move through the resistance area. If not, resistance can continue to hold.

This repeated-test behaviour is one of the structural characteristics considered by EdgeBreak scanners.

Support and resistance are zones

Beginners often draw support and resistance as exact horizontal lines. Real markets are less precise.

Buying and selling interest is usually spread across a range of prices, so support and resistance are often better treated as zones rather than one exact number.

Small movements above or below a level can occur without meaning that the entire structure has changed. Context matters.

Role reversal

Resistance can sometimes become support after price moves above it and begins trading on the other side of the former resistance zone.

Likewise, support that breaks can sometimes become resistance during a later recovery.

This behaviour is often called role reversal and can help explain how market structure changes over time.

It is not guaranteed to occur, but it is a useful concept to recognise when reviewing a chart.

Lesson summary

Support and resistance provide important reference areas for understanding price behaviour. Repeated tests, changing structure and the way price reacts around those zones can all add useful context, but no level guarantees what will happen next.

Key takeaways

  • Support marks an area where buying interest has previously influenced price.
  • Resistance marks an area where selling interest has repeatedly appeared.
  • Repeated resistance tests provide more context than a single test.
  • Support and resistance are usually better viewed as zones.
  • Resistance is a core part of EdgeBreak's technical research process.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Drawing support and resistance as exact prices rather than zones.
  • Assuming every move through resistance will continue.
  • Ignoring the broader market structure.
  • Relying on levels without considering volume or higher lows.
  • Forcing support or resistance where no clear reaction exists.
