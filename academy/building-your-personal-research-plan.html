Why create a research plan?

Markets generate an enormous amount of information every day. Without a structured routine, it is easy to become distracted by headlines, social media, rapidly moving prices and constantly changing opinions.

A research plan gives you a repeatable starting point. Instead of deciding what to do each time you open EdgeBreak, you follow the same broad sequence and focus on the same types of evidence.

Over time, consistency can improve pattern recognition and make it easier to compare current candidates with previous examples.

Your research plan does not need to be complicated. It needs to be simple enough that you can follow it consistently.

Your daily routine

A daily research session does not need to take hours. A shorter, focused process can be more useful than continuously jumping between charts without a clear purpose.

A typical EdgeBreak routine might include:

  1. Review the Automated Daily Scan and begin with the top-six researched shortlist.
  2. Use the NASDAQ Scanner, Breakout Scanner, Pre-Breakout Scanner or Launch Pad when you want to explore beyond the daily shortlist.
  3. Drag the strongest candidates into TradingView and compare weekly and daily structure.
  4. Compare resistance, higher lows, volume, price position and unusual market activity.
  5. Use AI Company Research for deeper company context and AI Chart Analysis for a descriptive second view of the chart.
  6. Use Guided AI or AI Chat when you need to clarify terminology, explore a research question or continue a stock-specific discussion.
  7. Save only the strongest research candidates to My Workspace.

The important part is not completing every possible analysis. It is following a repeatable sequence that keeps your attention on useful evidence.

Choose the right AI tool for the question

Your research plan does not need to use every AI feature on every stock. Use the tool that matches the question you are trying to answer.

  • AI Company Research — for deeper company, financial, news, filing and risk context.
  • AI Chart Analysis — for a descriptive review of visible technical structure.
  • Guided AI — for structured prompts and common research questions.
  • AI Chat — for flexible follow-up questions and stock-specific discussion.

The purpose is to reduce repetitive research work while keeping your own judgement and verification at the centre of the process.

Weekly watchlist reviews

Once each week, revisit the stocks saved in My Workspace.

Ask whether the original research characteristics are still present:

  • Has the broader market structure improved or weakened?
  • Are higher lows continuing?
  • Has resistance changed?
  • How has volume developed?
  • Has unusual market activity changed?
  • Does the stock still deserve further research?

A focused watchlist should evolve as the evidence changes. There is little value in keeping a stock simply because it was once interesting.

Monthly reflection

At the end of each month, review the quality of your research process rather than focusing only on individual stocks or market outcomes.

Useful questions include:

  • Did I follow my research routine consistently?
  • Which charts taught me the most?
  • Which structures am I recognising more easily?
  • Where did I make assumptions instead of following the evidence?
  • What is one part of the process I can improve next month?

This type of reflection can turn ordinary market observation into a much stronger learning process over time.

Keep your watchlist focused

A larger watchlist is not automatically a better watchlist.

Maintain a manageable group of stocks that you can realistically revisit and understand. Every saved stock should have a clear research reason for remaining there.

This helps prevent My Workspace from becoming another noisy list of tickers and keeps attention on the candidates with the strongest current evidence.

Document what you learn

Simple research notes can become one of your most useful long-term resources.

Record observations such as:

  • Changes in market structure.
  • Resistance behaviour.
  • Higher lows.
  • Volume behaviour.
  • Unusual market activity context.
  • Why the stock remains on your watchlist.
  • What evidence would make you reassess the original interpretation.

Over time, those notes create a personal library of examples that can be compared with future market structures.

Your repeatable research cycle

Discover → investigate → organise → review.

01 Discover Start with the Automated Daily Scan top six or use the manual scanners to explore further.
02 Investigate Review TradingView charts, market activity, company research and AI-assisted context.
03 Organise Save the strongest research candidates to My Workspace.
04 Review Revisit your evidence and refine the process as experience grows.

Avoid constantly changing your process

One common mistake is switching research methods every time the market becomes difficult or a particular setup does not work as expected.

Any research framework needs time and repetition before you can judge which parts are useful and which parts need improvement.

Consistency does not mean refusing to change. It means making changes because repeated evidence supports them rather than because of one emotional reaction.

Your research plan will evolve

As your experience grows, your research plan will naturally become more personal.

You may find that certain market structures, scanner filters, timeframes or areas of company research are more useful to you than others.

The objective is not to create a perfect routine immediately. It is to build a disciplined process that can be refined as your understanding develops.

Practical exercise

Create your own EdgeBreak research plan.

Write down a routine you can realistically follow.

  • Decide when you will complete your daily research session.
  • Write the exact order in which you will use EdgeBreak tools.
  • Schedule one weekly watchlist review.
  • Set aside time each month to review your overall research process.
  • Choose what observations you will record for each saved stock.
  • Follow the plan consistently before deciding what should change.

Lesson summary

A personal research plan creates structure, consistency and organisation. Using the Automated Daily Scan, scanners, TradingView, AI-assisted research, My Workspace, weekly watchlist review and monthly reflection can turn EdgeBreak into a repeatable process that improves with experience.

Key takeaways

  • Build a research routine you can realistically follow.
  • Keep your watchlist focused.
  • Record observations instead of relying on memory.
  • Review your process regularly.
  • Allow your research plan to evolve gradually with evidence and experience.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Randomly browsing the market without a process.
  • Keeping too many stocks on the watchlist.
  • Changing research methods after one disappointing outcome.
  • Failing to record why a stock was originally saved.
  • Spending more time searching for new ideas than reviewing existing research.
