Platform update

The Smart Money Filter is no longer a separate EdgeBreak tool.

Earlier versions of EdgeBreak allowed members to manually apply a Smart Money Filter to scanner results. That workflow has been retired.

The underlying idea has evolved into something more useful: market activity evidence is now gathered automatically in the background and incorporated into the Automated Daily Scan's ranking process.

Members no longer need to switch a filter on or choose a separate Smart Money screen. The analysis is part of the research pipeline itself.

Why EdgeBreak changed the approach

A separate filter placed too much emphasis on one category of evidence.

EdgeBreak now takes a broader approach. Technical structure, price behaviour, volume, unusual market activity and company-level research can be considered together rather than forcing members to decide whether one additional filter should be enabled.

This creates a more consistent process and keeps the member focused on the final research evidence rather than the mechanics behind every individual data source.

The goal is no longer “turn on Smart Money.” The goal is to combine several independent pieces of evidence before deciding which stocks deserve the most research attention.

What EdgeBreak now looks for

The updated process studies market activity that may be difficult to recognise from a standard price chart alone.

Two important areas include unusual activity occurring away from the primary exchange and larger purchase activity appearing across multiple markets or trading venues.

When these patterns repeat alongside constructive technical structure, they can provide an additional reason for a stock to receive closer attention.

Off-exchange activity Activity outside the primary exchange EdgeBreak compares unusual activity away from the main exchange with the stock's own recent behaviour.
Cross-market purchases Large activity across more than one market Repeated larger purchases appearing across multiple markets can strengthen the case for deeper investigation.
Combined evidence One layer of a larger model Market activity is considered alongside technical structure, trend, volume and other research evidence.

Built into the top-six process

The most important change is where this information is used.

EdgeBreak's Automated Daily Scan narrows thousands of NASDAQ-listed stocks through multiple stages of filtering and research. Market activity evidence is now incorporated before the final research shortlist is surfaced.

This means unusual off-exchange activity and larger cross-market purchase patterns can influence how the strongest candidates are prioritised for the final top six.

Members see the outcome of the combined process rather than needing to manually reproduce each stage themselves.

What is happening behind the shortlist?

The exact EdgeBreak ranking process is proprietary, but the principle is straightforward: stronger candidates should be supported by more than one form of evidence.

Technical structure Trend, resistance, higher lows, base quality and distance from the underlying structure.
Market participation Volume behaviour and whether participation appears unusual relative to recent history.
Off-exchange evidence Activity away from the primary exchange that stands out against the stock's normal pattern.
Cross-market evidence Larger purchase activity appearing across multiple markets rather than in only one isolated location.

Why multiple markets matter

A single unusual transaction can occur for many reasons.

When larger purchase activity appears repeatedly across different markets or venues, it provides a broader pattern for EdgeBreak to investigate.

The value comes from repetition and agreement between several sources of evidence, not from treating one large transaction as a definitive signal.

What this does not prove

Even strong market activity evidence does not reveal the complete identity, motive or future intentions of every participant.

EdgeBreak therefore avoids presenting these observations as confirmed institutional accumulation or guaranteed buying pressure.

Instead, they are treated as another research layer that can strengthen or weaken the case for further investigation.

How members should use the information

You do not need to recreate the old Smart Money Filter workflow.

Start with the Automated Daily Scan and review the top-six research candidates that survive the complete process. Then move into the chart, company research and your own watchlist.

The member-facing workflow stays simple even though more evidence is being considered in the background.

Continue with chart research

Once a candidate reaches your research list, open the chart and review the same technical evidence taught throughout the Academy:

  • Broader trend.
  • Resistance and support.
  • Higher lows.
  • Range quality.
  • Volume behaviour.
  • Whether price remains close to the original structure.

Market activity evidence should support chart research, not replace it.

Save candidates for ongoing review

If the complete evidence still deserves attention, save the stock to My Workspace.

This allows you to revisit the candidate and compare later market behaviour with the evidence that originally caused it to stand out.

Practical exercise

Review the top six as combined evidence.

Open the latest Automated Daily Scan and choose three of the surfaced candidates.

  • Review the technical structure of each stock.
  • Compare resistance, higher lows and volume.
  • Note any unusual market activity context presented by EdgeBreak.
  • Use AI Company Research for one candidate.
  • Decide which candidate has the clearest combination of evidence.
  • Save that stock to My Workspace for later review.

Lesson summary

The Smart Money Filter is no longer used as a separate EdgeBreak filter. Its original purpose has evolved into built-in market activity analysis that examines unusual off-exchange activity and larger purchase patterns appearing across multiple markets as part of the Automated Daily Scan's top-six ranking process.

Key takeaways

  • The old Smart Money Filter has been retired.
  • Market activity analysis now runs automatically in the background.
  • EdgeBreak studies unusual off-exchange activity.
  • Large purchases across multiple markets can add supporting evidence.
  • This evidence contributes to prioritising the top-six research candidates.
  • No individual data point proves institutional buying or future price direction.

Common misunderstandings

  • Looking for a Smart Money toggle that no longer exists.
  • Assuming one large transaction proves accumulation.
  • Ignoring the technical chart because unusual activity is present.
  • Treating the top six as buy recommendations.
  • Assuming the ranking guarantees future performance.
✦

Research reminder

Off-exchange activity and large cross-market purchases are used as research evidence only. They do not establish the identity or intentions of market participants and should not be interpreted as confirmed institutional buying, financial advice, investment recommendations or predictions of future market performance.
