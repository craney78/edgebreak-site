What is market structure?

Market structure describes how price behaves over time through the sequence of highs, lows, trends and consolidations. Instead of focusing on one candle, it looks at how the broader chart is developing across days, weeks or months.

This makes it easier to describe whether a stock is trending higher, trending lower or moving sideways inside a range.

Why structure matters before a breakout

Many investors first notice a stock when price makes a large move. Market structure provides context before that event occurs.

A stock forming higher lows beneath clearly defined resistance is technically different from a stock approaching resistance through wide, erratic swings and repeated structural failures.

The key idea

Read the sequence.
Not just the candle.

Market structure is about relationships: where highs are forming, where lows are holding, how support and resistance interact and whether the range is becoming more organised or less organised.

Weakening structure

A weaker technical picture often appears when rallies lose strength and support becomes less reliable.

—
Lower highs begin replacing previous peaks.
—
Lower lows or repeated support failures develop.
—
Price becomes increasingly volatile and disorganised.
—
Breakout attempts repeatedly fail to hold.

Higher highs and higher lows

An uptrend is commonly described by a sequence of higher highs and higher lows. Each new rally reaches above the previous high, while pullbacks remain above the previous low.

This does not prove who is buying or guarantee that the trend will continue. It simply describes an improving price structure.

Lower highs and lower lows

A downtrend develops when rallies fail below previous highs and declines move through previous lows. The sequence shows that the market is producing progressively weaker price levels.

Breakout research becomes more difficult when the broader structure remains weak because the stock may still be fighting an established downward trend.

Consolidation can be constructive

Markets rarely move in a straight line. Stocks often spend time trading sideways after an advance or decline.

When consolidation becomes orderly, with clearly defined support and resistance and reduced volatility, it can create a useful base for further research.

Support and resistance create reference points

Support and resistance help define the boundaries of a structure. Repeated reactions around similar areas can make those zones useful when monitoring how price is changing over time.

The important point is that these are areas of repeated market behaviour rather than perfectly exact prices.

Structure should be combined with other evidence

Market structure is only one layer of research. Volume, broader market conditions, recent company developments, financial information and unusual market activity can all add context.

Final thoughts

Understanding market structure helps investors move beyond individual candles and consider how price is behaving over a longer sequence.

Higher highs, higher lows, lower highs, lower lows, support, resistance and consolidation can all help describe whether a stock is strengthening, weakening or becoming more balanced.

The strongest use of market structure is descriptive rather than predictive. It gives the investor a clearer framework for deciding which stocks deserve continued research.
