What is a NASDAQ stock scanner?

A NASDAQ stock scanner is software that searches NASDAQ-listed securities using predefined criteria. Instead of manually opening thousands of charts, the scanner processes market data and identifies stocks that match the selected research rules.

This creates a much smaller group of candidates that can then be investigated in more detail.

Why use a stock scanner?

More than 3,200 stocks are listed on the NASDAQ. Even a very quick manual review of every chart would consume a large amount of time and attention.

Automated scanning moves the repetitive filtering work to the computer so the investor can focus on the stocks whose current structure actually deserves further research.

The key idea

Let the scanner search.
Keep the research with you.

A scanner solves the discovery problem. It can narrow thousands of stocks into a manageable shortlist, but it does not determine whether any stock should be bought, sold or held.

Finding breakout stocks

One common use of a NASDAQ stock scanner is breakout discovery. Automated rules can identify stocks interacting with resistance, developing higher lows or already moving through an established resistance area.

The scanner does not know whether that breakout will succeed. Its role is to identify stocks whose current structure matches the research criteria.

Finding stocks before a breakout

Scanning can also begin earlier. Pre-breakout research focuses on stocks that remain below resistance while support, higher lows and consolidation are developing.

This can provide more time to investigate the company and monitor the technical structure before a confirmed move occurs.

Base-forming stocks

Not every useful research candidate needs to be immediately below resistance. Some stocks are still building organised bases inside established trading ranges.

Launch Pad research focuses on this earlier stage, where support, resistance and the quality of the trading range can be monitored as the structure develops.

Scanning is only the beginning

Finding a technically interesting stock is only the first stage of the process. Investors may also want to understand the company, recent financial performance, news, filings, market activity and the chart itself.

A more complete workflow moves from stock discovery into independent company and technical research rather than treating scanner results as signals.

Final thoughts

A NASDAQ stock scanner can make market research far more efficient by automatically reducing thousands of stocks into a manageable shortlist.

Its value is not in predicting the next winner. The value is in applying consistent rules to a large market and showing the investor which stocks currently deserve more attention.

Once the shortlist exists, company research, chart analysis and independent verification can take over from the scanner.
